Cover vs Interior Design: What's the Difference?

Authors often ask what the difference between cover design and interior design actually is, and it's a fair question, because the two disciplines get bundled together so often. Put simply: the cover sells the book. It's the first impression, the thing that stops someone scrolling or browsing long enough to pick your book up. The interior sells the reading experience. Typography, margins, chapter heading style and white space decide whether reading your book feels effortless or exhausting for three hundred pages straight.

They're connected disciplines, not the same job. A cover can be stunning and still sit awkwardly against an interior that ignores genre expectations, trim size or readability. We design both as part of one visual system, so the promise your cover makes to a reader is the one your interior actually delivers.

Book Layout & Interior Design Services

Book Layout Design Australia is the discipline of turning a manuscript into pages a reader can actually enjoy. It covers chapter heading styles, margins, gutter width, running heads, page numbers and the white space around all of it, with every decision matched to genre and trim size rather than applied as a generic default.

Book Interior Design Australia work is genre led too. A literary novel wants generous margins and a restrained chapter opener. Trim Size & Genre: Where Layout Decisions Start

Trim size, the physical page height and width of your printed book, isn't a technical afterthought. It's a genre decision that affects your cover proportions and your interior layout together, which is why we consider book trim size by genre before any design work starts.

YA and commercial fiction often use a larger trim size to keep page count down and shelf presence up. Literary fiction tends to run smaller and more intimate. Cookbooks, photography books and illustrated non fiction usually need a larger, sometimes square format to give images room to breathe. Get trim size wrong and you're fighting it at every later stage, whether that's a cover that doesn't fit the format or an interior with awkward margins and cramped images. We choose trim size early, as a joint cover and interior decision, not a printer setting picked at the last minute.

Can AI Design My Book Cover?

Yes, and honestly, a lot of authors try. AI tools can generate cover concepts fast, and they're genuinely useful for exploring visual direction or mood before committing to a design brief.

Where AI tools usually fall short is the finishing. Files generated by AI aren't typically upload ready for Amazon KDP or IngramSpark — spine width, bleed, resolution and CMYK colour all need setting up correctly for print, and AI generated images often aren't licensed for commercial book cover use or high resolution output. There's also the composition problem: covers need specific space for title, author name and back cover text, which most AI tools don't account for.

Our take: use AI for inspiration if it helps you think through direction. For a cover you can actually publish, professional finishing still matters.
